## The claim

> "Krill oil is better than fish oil."

The new human trial makes this claim more understandable — but still not cleanly established. The paper shows that krill oil raised **plasma omega-3 levels** more than fish oil in healthy adults. That is a meaningful signal about **bioavailability**. It is not the same thing as proven superiority for cardiovascular outcomes, inflammation, cognition, or overall health.

## What the evidence actually shows

### What supports the claim

The new study is methodologically much better than typical supplement marketing: a **double-blind randomized controlled trial** with a direct head-to-head comparison. That is exactly how product forms should be tested.

The result: krill oil increased circulating omega-3 fatty acids more than fish oil. That supports the idea that krill oil may be **absorbed more efficiently or distributed differently** in this setting. For consumers, that is not trivial, because krill oil is often marketed as more bioavailable — and this study does provide a real human signal in that direction.

### Where the claim goes too far

The jump from **higher blood levels** to **better health outcomes** is the critical problem.

- The study mainly measures a **surrogate marker**: plasma omega-3 levels.
- It does **not directly prove** that krill oil prevents heart attacks better, lowers triglycerides more, reduces inflammation more, or supports brain health better than fish oil.
- Even if krill oil is more bioavailable, that does not automatically mean the real-world clinical benefit is meaningfully greater.
- Price, dose, EPA/DHA composition, and the exact comparator product all matter.

In short: the paper strengthens the narrower claim that **krill oil may be more bioavailable**. It does not yet justify the stronger claim that **krill oil is clinically better than fish oil**.

## EFSA / regulatory status

In the EU, approved health claims relate to **EPA and DHA** and their contribution to normal physiological functions such as heart function or brain function. There is no approved claim that **krill oil as a product form is superior to fish oil**.

## Verdict

**Exaggerated.** The new RCT is good enough to take krill oil more seriously than pure premium-marketing hype. But what it mainly shows is a **possible bioavailability advantage**, not clearly proven clinical superiority. Turning this paper into “krill oil is better than fish oil” goes one step further than the data currently support.
