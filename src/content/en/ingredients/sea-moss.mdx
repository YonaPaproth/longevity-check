## Mechanism of Action

Chondrus crispus (Irish Sea Moss) is a red algae traditionally used as a thickening agent in Irish and Caribbean cuisine. It contains naturally occurring minerals (iodine, iron, zinc, magnesium) and carrageenan (a polysaccharide). The virally promoted effects on thyroid, immune system, and gut are based on mineral content, not specific mechanisms of action.

## Evidence

There are **no RCTs** on Irish Sea Moss as a dietary supplement. Evidence is limited to nutrient analyses (mineral content) and in-vitro studies on carrageenan. Social media claims ("92 of 102 essential minerals") are scientifically unsubstantiated and misleading.

## Safety

**Main risks:**
- **High iodine content** — can trigger hyperthyroidism, especially in those with Hashimoto's or Graves' disease
- **Heavy metals** — algae accumulate lead, arsenic, and cadmium from seawater
- **Carrageenan debate** — degraded carrageenan is pro-inflammatory in animal models

Not recommended for thyroid conditions, pregnancy, or anticoagulant use.

## Dosage

If desired despite the above: 1000–2400 mg per day. Check thyroid levels before and after 4 weeks of use.
