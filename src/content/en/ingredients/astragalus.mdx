## What is Astragalus?

Astragalus membranaceus (commonly called Huang Qi in traditional Chinese medicine) is a legume root long used in Asian herbal medicine for purported immune support and longevity. It has entered Western supplement markets riding on both traditional reputation and modern marketing linking it to telomeres, immunosenescence reversal, and healthy aging.

## Mechanisms proposed

Astragalus contains several bioactive compounds (polysaccharides, saponins, flavonoids) that in cell and animal models show:

- **Immune modulation:** Stimulation of macrophage activity, enhanced NK cell function, and Th1 shift in preclinical systems.
- **Telomerase and telomere support:** Some in vitro data suggest astragalus extracts can activate telomerase in certain cell types—a narrative that has heavily colored supplement marketing.
- **Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects:** Via polysaccharide and saponin fractions.

## What does human evidence show?

- **Clinical trials in humans are sparse and often small.** Most positive studies come from China or are published in lower-impact journals; quality and blinding vary widely.
- **Immune markers:** A few small trials report improvements in lymphocyte counts or IgA levels in older adults or immunocompromised groups (e.g., patients on chemotherapy). Effect sizes and clinical significance are unclear.
- **Telomere and longevity data:** Despite heavy marketing, there is essentially **no rigorous human data** showing that Astragalus supplementation lengthens telomeres, alters telomerase activity in vivo, or improves lifespan. Telomere claims rest entirely on mechanistic extrapolation and cell culture.
- **Healthy population studies:** No large RCTs in healthy volunteers examining immune function, infection rates, or aging markers.

## Regulatory status

- No EFSA health claims approved for Astragalus.
- Marketing claims linking Astragalus to "immune boosting," "anti-aging," or "telomere support" are not evidence-based per EU standards.

## Safety

Astragalus is generally **well-tolerated** at standard doses (500–2000 mg/day of extract):
- Mild GI effects occasionally reported.
- No major toxicity signals in safety studies.
- Traditional use in Asia spans centuries with a favorable safety profile.

## Bottom line

Astragalus is an interesting traditional herb with plausible immune mechanisms in preclinical systems. However, human clinical evidence is sparse, and telomere/longevity claims are speculative at best. If you have recurrent infections or are interested in immune support, Astragalus *might* be worth experimenting with—but recognize that the evidence is far weaker than marketing suggests. For proven immune function, prioritize sleep, exercise, adequate nutrition, stress management, and vaccination over herbal supplements.
